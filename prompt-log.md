## Reflection
When I tested the value of relaxing the carrot cap, Codex's first instruction was to change the input cap from 20 to 21 and rerun Solver. I did that, and the result stayed at 10/20/30 with no change in profit — the model hadn't actually tested a 21st carrot bed. Codex's initial guidance was incomplete: changing the input assumption alone did nothing, because Solver's own constraint was hard-coded at 20 in Solver's settings rather than linked to the input cell. I only caught this by comparing the unchanged Solver output against what I expected to see and asking why nothing had moved. Once we located the hard-coded constraint, I temporarily changed the grouped tomato/carrot constraint to 21 while tomatoes remained at 10. I then reran Solver, and got 10/21/30 at $43,114 — a $352 profit increase. I ran the same check for mesclun, raising its Solver constraint from 30 to 31, and got 10/20/31 at $43,008 — a $246 increase. In both cases I restored the original caps afterward and reran the baseline to confirm it still returned 10/20/30 at $42,762 before saving the workbook.

This mattered because I could have mistaken the unchanged profit for evidence that relaxing the carrot cap had no value. Instead, reporting the unchanged result led us to inspect Solver's settings and discover that the intended constraint had never changed. The same discipline applied to the marginal-cost readings elsewhere in the model, where I corrected at least one reading (carrot MC) that I had transcribed wrong. Codex helped explain the results, but I verified the cap-relaxation gains by rerunning Solver and checked the marginal-cost figures against their worksheet cells. Dollar figures in the analysis are reported to the cent where exact values are discussed. Figure labels are rounded to the nearest dollar for readability.
