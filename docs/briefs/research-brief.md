## Proposed paper question

How much of a 24/7 healthcare facility’s increase in blended electricity rate across its April–July 2026 statements is accounted for by the Energy Cost Recovery adjustment versus other billed charges? Based on that breakdown and the estimated costs and savings of LED conversion, should the company President approve the conversion to reduce grid electricity purchases without reducing clinical availability?

## Current evidence

- The facility’s records show grid electricity use declining across the April–July 2026 statements while the blended electricity rate rises from approximately $0.32 to $0.44 per kWh. These statement labels cover service from February 21 through June 22, 2026.
- A preliminary bill decomposition has been developed with AI assistance. The line-item transcription, calculations, and classification of charges still require my verification against the original statements.
- The analysis will distinguish changes in Energy Cost Recovery from changes in other charges per kWh, including the effect of spreading customer and demand charges over fewer kWh.
- A potential vendor’s LED estimate provides a proposed response to evaluate. Project cost, rebates, avoided electricity charges, and any reduction in billed peak demand need verification before finalizing the payback estimate.

## Response to review and planned checks

Following Adam’s review, I narrowed the question from attributing electricity costs to the geopolitical oil shock to measuring the facility’s exposure through its billed charges.

The following checks are being documented after preliminary analysis, rather than before seeing the data:

- If Energy Cost Recovery per kWh is roughly flat while the blended rate rises, that would weaken the explanation that this adjustment drove the increase. I would examine other charges and the effect of falling consumption.
- I will compare the facility’s Energy Cost Recovery per kWh with HECO’s published adjustments, accounting for the actual service dates and any adjustments that change within a billing period. A mismatch would require investigation before drawing a conclusion about the transmission channel.

The recommendation will evaluate whether the company President should approve LED conversion using estimated project cost and avoided electricity charges while maintaining clinical availability.

## Proposed investment decision criteria

The earlier 4.5-year payback target was provisional and based on preliminary calculations. This evaluation proposes replacing it with a five-year analysis of savings, net of replacement and other incremental costs. Product lives and warranties informed the five-year period. They help set the evaluation window, but they do not establish what financial return the facility should require. To test that uncertainty, I will discount net savings at assumed annual returns of 5%, 8%, and 10%. These rates are analytical assumptions, not facility policy. Under the proposed criteria, the investment qualifies if its discounted net savings cover its net cost, subject to the President accepting these criteria. Fixture counts and wattages are not yet available, so I will use assumed savings scenarios based on the vendor estimate until those figures are verified.
